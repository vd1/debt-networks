What Sigma showed
YTD

YTD maps what yield depends on, recursively.

The output is not a vault label or a stablecoin label. It is a live dependency tree across allocations, collateral, markets, wrappers, queues, redemption paths, and off-chain boundaries.

The call emphasized a practical point: once the tree exists, risk propagation, exit analysis, and high-cadence monitoring can sit on top.

YTD view: NAV decomposed to source, with underlying markets, rates, contribution, and collateral metadata.

Technical split
Adapters

The clean split is token/vault standards on one side, yield traversal on the other.

Both teams already think in adapters. The call clarified that new vaults and protocols will still require specific integration work.

The joint product should not flatten these differences. It should make each adapter responsible for the part it can verify best.

Adapter responsibilities |- Bluprynt | |- token standards | |- vault standards | |- off-chain disclosures | `- attestation semantics `- Sigma |- protocol traversal |- yield recursion |- exit markets `- risk propagation
The merge
Graph join, not dashboard glue

The shared product is a graph join, not two dashboards beside each other.

Sigma finds the live dependency and risk path. Bluprynt attaches the issuer, disclosure, credential, custody, collateral, and revocation state behind the off-chain node.

On-chain path

Vault, lending, collateral, nested token, liquidation, and exit-market dependencies.

Off-chain claim

Issuer, legal wrapper, collateral description, redemption rights, and custody context.

Monitored state

Freshness, revocation, attestation source, and reporting status become risk inputs.
