\subsection{Cross-drawer patterns worth pulling out}\label{cross-drawer-patterns-worth-pulling-out}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{DPRK / Lazarus attribution on the big ones} --- Drift (\$285M), KelpDAO-related (\$292M operational attribution debated), Zerion (\$100K), Upbit CeFi (\$36M Nov 2025). The pattern: increasingly patient, AI-assisted SE against signers rather than direct contract attacks.
\item
  \textbf{Archeological contracts} --- Truebit (2021 code), Aevo legacy Ribbon, Yearn iEarn v1. Forks and legacy deployments carry tail risk for years.
\item
  \textbf{\texttt{.fi} / DNS-registrar weakness as a campaign} --- CoW Swap + Steakhouse + HypurrFi + Neutrl. Registrar hardening (RegistryLock, DNSSEC) is the cheap mitigation.
\item
  \textbf{Curator concentration \textgreater{} diversity} --- Stream, Resolv, Elixir all show that ``vault N has exposure to asset X'' aggregated across curators produces concentrated single-asset risk even when any individual vault looks small. Directly validates the \textbf{TVL illusion} and \textbf{diversification illusion} entries in \texttt{QFTaskList.tex}.
\item
  \textbf{Oracle-pinning as a cascade amplifier} --- Hardcoding \$1 on a depegging asset converts a price-discovery event into unrecoverable bad debt. Stream is the textbook case.
\end{enumerate}
