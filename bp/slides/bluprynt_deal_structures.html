Option 1
Full ownership by Bluprynt Internal · Sigma only

Treat this as an acquisition, not a partnership.

If Bluprynt owns t1 + t2, Sigma is handing over its core asset for startup-partnership economics.

Why this is dangerous

BP may fail within the exclusivity period, taking the best use of t2 with it.
They have modest seed funding relative to US salaries and enterprise sales cycles.
Ownership could block Sigma from selling to curators, its natural customer base.
Any future acquirer of BP could inherit control over Sigma's core methodology.

Only accept this path for acquisition-level consideration, strong cash at close, and explicit freedom for Sigma founders to keep working in m2.

Option 2
No special footing Internal · Sigma only

This preserves optionality, but m1 optionality may be hard to realize alone.

What works

Sigma keeps t2 completely unencumbered.
Sigma can sell to BP competitors or future RWA platforms.
No market carve-out, no dependency on BP execution.

What is weak

Regulators and issuers buy trust, legal workflow, compliance surface, and institutional references.
Sigma's direct network is m2, not m1.
Competitors may exist but may not feel urgent need for t2 until the market matures.

Economics
Make exclusivity earned Shareable with Bluprynt

If BP wants Sigma not to sell t2 into m1, they pay for the lost option value.

Paid pilot

60-90 days around Plume/Nest or PYUSD/syrupUSDC. Non-exclusive until the license option is exercised.

Annual floor

Minimum research and license fee. Exclusivity exists only while the floor is paid.

Revenue share

Royalty on insurance-tier or premium revenue enabled by Sigma models. Sigma keeps a majority channel share — target 70% — on Sigma-originated accounts.

Missed payment, no launch, insolvency, or no covered customer conversion should automatically end exclusivity.
